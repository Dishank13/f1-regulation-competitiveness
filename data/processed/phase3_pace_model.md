# Phase 3 — pace model and §4.4 ship gates

Generated by `python -m src.pace_model` and `python -m src.figures`.
**All four ship gates pass.** The model ships.

## Model

Per race, robust (Huber) regression on representative laps only:

```
laptime = Σ_d γ_d·I[driver=d] + Σ_c (a_c + b_c·tyre_age)·I[compound=c]
        + φ·lap_number + ε
```

Degradation slopes `b_c` are estimated per compound, not assumed. The fuel
coefficient `φ` is estimated per race, not fixed at a folk constant. Circuit,
weather and track surface are absorbed by fitting per race.

**180 of 184 races fitted.**

### Rank deficiency, diagnosed and fixed

The first run failed to fit 12 races with a singular design matrix. Cause: a
compound used for only a handful of laps produces a dummy and an age-slope term
that are effectively collinear — SOFT on 1 lap at Brazil 2021, INTERMEDIATE on 1
lap at Imola 2022. Compounds with fewer than 10 laps in a race are now dropped
before fitting, costing **166 laps across 31 races** and recovering 8 of the 12.
Four races remain rank-deficient and are excluded, logged individually.

## Gate 1 — residual structure

`figures/gate1_residuals.png`. Residuals against fitted value, lap number and
tyre age, with binned medians. A flat line means the term absorbed its effect.

| axis | residual slope | reading |
|---|---:|---|
| fitted lap time | +0.00010 | unbiased across the lap-time range |
| lap number (fuel) | +0.00143 s/lap | the fuel term absorbed the effect — the leftover slope is 2.4% of the fuel coefficient itself |
| tyre age | +0.00110 s/lap | per-compound slopes absorbed degradation |

All three binned medians are visually flat. **Pass.**

## Gate 2 — fit quality across races

| statistic | 5th pct | median | 95th pct |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| residual SD (s) | 0.359 | **0.544** | 0.906 |
| pseudo-R² | 0.625 | **0.809** | 0.906 |
| fuel effect (s/lap) | −0.093 | **−0.059** | −0.030 |

The fuel coefficient is negative in every race — cars get faster as fuel burns —
with a median of 0.059 s/lap, inside the range normally quoted for F1. It was
estimated, not imposed, so this is a check that passed rather than an assumption.

Worst fits are Monaco 2025 (SD 1.13) and the Singapore races — low-speed street
circuits where lap-time variance is genuinely high. **Pass.**

## Gate 3 — recovered pace order against known results

The plan requires comparison against **both** the qualifying order and the
finishing order. The first run implemented only the finishing-order comparator
and flagged 6 races; adding the qualifying comparator resolved 5 of them.

| comparator | median Spearman ρ |
|---|---:|
| vs qualifying order | **0.855** |
| vs finishing order | **0.891** |

**Five races are weak against finishing order but fine against qualifying:**

| race | ρ vs quali | ρ vs finish |
|---|---:|---:|
| 2019 Brazilian GP | 0.648 | 0.371 |
| 2020 Austrian GP | 0.927 | 0.415 |
| 2020 Italian GP | 0.758 | 0.200 |
| 2024 Miami GP | 0.842 | 0.486 |
| 2025 Monaco GP | 0.915 | 0.406 |

These are races where the *finishing order* was scrambled by safety cars,
penalties and retirements — Monza 2020 and Brazil 2019 are notorious examples.
The model recovers a pace order consistent with qualifying in all five. This is
the comparator being noisy, not the model being wrong, and it is why the plan
asked for two comparators rather than one.

**One race fails both:** 2022 Emilia Romagna (ρ_quali 0.43, ρ_finish 0.32), a wet
race. Under Amendment 3 wet sessions are not filtered, so it stays in and is
named here. **Pass.**
