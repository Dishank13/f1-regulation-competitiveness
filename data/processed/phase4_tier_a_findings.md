### 2.1 But the controls failed, and that changes the interpretation

Plan §4.1.2 stated the bias signature would be "a step in the front-of-field
metrics that the whole-field metrics do not show." **The whole-field metrics show
it too, and more strongly in relative terms:**

| metric | 2010 shift ÷ median abs reset shift |
|---|---:|
| M2 IQR (control) | **1.93×** |
| M1 sd (control) | 1.46× |
| M4 front gap | 1.35× |
| M5 front pair | 0.21× |

M2, a designated control, has the *largest* relative 2010 step of any metric.

So the 2010 discontinuity is **not specific to front-of-field measurement**, and
the D1c design cannot attribute it to segment-eligibility bias. At least three
candidate causes are collinear at 2010 and none is separable here:

1. **Grid composition.** The grid went from 10 constructors in 2009 to 12 in
   2010, and the three entrants (HRT, Lotus Racing, Virgin) were new
   backmarkers. M6 backmarker gap moves 1.664 → 6.122, a 3.7× jump. This alone
   would produce most of the observed step in every dispersion metric.
2. **The refuelling ban**, already a flagged discontinuity, which changed
   qualifying from race-fuel to low-fuel across the whole field.
3. **Segment eligibility** (§4.1.1), the effect D1c was built to detect.

**The restriction on M4 stands regardless.** It was pre-committed on the observed
magnitude, not on a diagnosis of cause, and it is not weakened by our now having
a competing explanation. Reverse-engineering an exemption after seeing the result
is exactly what pre-committing was for.

## 3. Two findings recorded, not yet interpreted

### 3.1 A non-reset season produces larger metric shifts than reset boundaries do

The 2010 step exceeds the *median absolute reset-boundary shift* on M1, M2 and
M4 — by 1.5× to 1.9×. 2010 is not a reset. This is the placebo logic arriving
early and pointing at the method: if ordinary season-to-season churn (chiefly
grid composition) moves these metrics more than regulation resets do, the
resets' estimates are being read against a noisy baseline.

Whether this undermines the headline or *is* the headline is Decision H and is
not settled here. It is flagged now because it will bear on Decision B: a metric
that is highly sensitive to grid composition is a poor primary in a study whose
window contains three grid expansions (2010, 2016, 2026).

### 3.2 M5 is at or below the driver-noise floor in recent seasons

Plan §4.3 requires M7 be reported alongside any best-of-team result, because a
between-team difference smaller than the prevailing within-team driver spread is
not interpretable as a car effect.

M7 sits at 0.27–0.46 throughout. **M5 (front-pair gap) is below M7 in most
seasons** — 2025: M5 = 0.237 against M7 = 0.297; 2026: M5 = 0.141 against
M7 = 0.280. In those seasons the gap between the two fastest cars is smaller than
the typical gap between two drivers in the same car.

M5 is therefore **not interpretable as a car-performance measure in the recent
era**, whatever its statistical significance. This is a measurement-floor
problem, not a power problem, and more data would not fix it.
