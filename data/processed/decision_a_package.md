## Knob 1 — traffic gap. Recommend **3.0 s**, not the provisional 1.0 s.

Rather than assert a value, I measured the lap-time penalty as a function of gap
to the car ahead, **within driver-stint cells** so that car, compound, tyre age
and fuel are approximately fixed and the residual variation is attributable to
traffic. Baseline is each stint's own median lap at gap ≥ 3 s.

| gap to car ahead (s) | laps | median penalty (s) |
|---|---:|---:|
| 0.00 – 0.25 | 484 | **+1.387** |
| 0.25 – 0.50 | 3,018 | +1.077 |
| 0.50 – 0.75 | 7,202 | +0.721 |
| 0.75 – 1.00 | 7,453 | +0.504 |
| **1.00 – 1.25** | 6,404 | **+0.380** |
| 1.25 – 1.50 | 6,276 | +0.301 |
| 1.50 – 2.00 | 11,105 | +0.210 |
| 2.00 – 2.50 | 9,498 | +0.113 |
| 2.50 – 3.00 | 8,149 | +0.076 |
| **3.00 – 4.00** | 13,162 | **0.000** |
| 4.00 – 6.00 | 16,946 | 0.000 |
| 6.00+ | 41,301 | ≈0.000 |

**The penalty reaches zero at 3.0 s, not 1.0 s.** At the provisional 1.0 s
threshold a median **+0.38 s** traffic penalty remains in the retained laps.

That number needs comparing to what we are trying to measure. The driver-noise
floor M7 runs 0.24–0.38 across Tier B seasons, and M5 runs 0.13–0.32.
**The residual traffic contamination at 1.0 s is larger than the signal in some
of the metrics.**

### The retention cost is much smaller than it appears

| gap | laps kept | % of base | residual penalty | team-races | races with ≥40 laps |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 1.0 s | 129,827 | 77.6% | +0.38 | 1,847 | 184 / 184 |
| 1.5 s | 112,763 | 67.4% | +0.21 | 1,845 | 184 / 184 |
| 2.0 s | 98,721 | 59.0% | +0.11 | 1,845 | 184 / 184 |
| 2.5 s | 87,759 | 52.5% | +0.08 | 1,840 | 184 / 184 |
| **3.0 s** | **79,153** | **47.3%** | **0.00** | **1,832** | **184 / 184** |
| 4.0 s | 66,315 | 39.7% | 0.00 | 1,815 | 184 / 184 |

**No race and essentially no team-race is lost.** Going from 1.0 s to 3.0 s costs
lap *count*, not *coverage*: every one of the 184 races still clears the model's
40-lap floor, and team-races fall only from 1,847 to 1,832. At 3.0 s the median
race still has **436** representative laps and the median team-race **41** —
ample for a per-race fit of roughly 20 driver coefficients.

### Why this is not a close call

Traffic exposure is **not random across the field**. Slower cars spend more time
in traffic, so leaving traffic in the data inflates their measured pace deficit —
and field dispersion is exactly the quantity being estimated. Worse, the amount
of traffic in a race is itself a function of how spread the field is, so the
contamination correlates with the outcome across seasons.

I recommended 1.0 s provisionally because it is the DRS activation distance. That
was reasoning from the wrong quantity: DRS range is a sporting rule about
overtaking aid, not a measurement of where aerodynamic wake stops mattering. The
curve says the wake matters out to about 3 s.

**Fallback if you want more data:** 2.5 s leaves +0.08 s and retains 52.5%. I
would not go below 2.0 s.

## Knob 2 — percent off own session best. Recommend **107%**, unchanged.

Distribution of each lap as a percentage of that driver's own session best:

| percentile | % of own best |
|---:|---:|
| 50 | 102.02 |
| 75 | 103.20 |
| 90 | 104.54 |
| 95 | 105.81 |
| **97** | **107.68** |
| 98 | 111.69 |
| 99 | 116.26 |

107% sits at roughly the **96.5th percentile**, right at the knee where the tail
begins. Below it the distribution is dense and continuous — a 103% cutoff would
remove 28.6% of laps, cutting deep into ordinary race pace. Above it the curve
flattens: 110% and 115% barely filter anything (97.7% and 98.7% retained).

The choice is not knife-edge — anything from 106% to 110% behaves similarly —
which is the best argument for keeping the value the sport itself uses.

## Knob 3 — minimum laps per team-race. Recommend **3**, not the provisional 5.

The lap-level sweep showed this knob is nearly inert. It is not inert in the way
that matters.

**Low-lap team-races are disproportionately slow teams:**

| laps in team-race | count | median rank in field (1.0 = slowest) |
|---|---:|---:|
| < 3 | 19 | **0.800** |
| 3 – 4 | 21 | 0.667 |
| 5 – 7 | 43 | 0.600 |
| 8 – 11 | 76 | 0.528 |
| 12 – 19 | 168 | 0.600 |
| 20+ | 1,505 | 0.500 |

Team-races dropped at k = 5 have a median field rank of **0.789**, against
**0.556** for all team-races.

This is a filter that **selectively removes backmarkers**. Removing the slowest
teams narrows measured field dispersion — which is the direction of the
hypothesis. A threshold that quietly makes the field look closer is the last
place to be generous.

| k | team-races dropped | % dropped |
|---:|---:|---:|
| 1 | 0 | 0.00 |
| **3** | **19** | **1.04** |
| 5 | 40 | 2.18 |
| 8 | 83 | 4.53 |
| 12 | 159 | 8.68 |
| 20 | 327 | 17.85 |

(at traffic gap 3.0 s)

The usual argument for a higher k is estimate noise, but the noise gradient is
weak and non-monotonic here — median SEM is 0.29 s for 3–4 laps, 0.21 s for 5–7,
0.26 s for 8–11, 0.13 s for 20+. There is no clean threshold where noise falls
away, so there is no noise-based reason to pay the selection cost.

**Recommend k = 3**, chosen on anti-bias grounds rather than noise grounds. k = 1
is also defensible; I would not go above 5.
